Meta Muse Alternatives: 7 Picks for People Who Can't (or Won't) Use Muse

Meta Muse is US only and trains on your chats by default. Seven alternatives, from open source agents that run on your own computer to hosted ones from OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI.

CG Chaitanya Giri · Sep 26, 2026 · 5 min read Comparisons
TL;DR

For a personal assistant you own, try OpenClaw. For a team of work agents on your own computer, try Munder Difflin. For a hosted assistant from a big lab, try ChatGPT Work, Gemini Spark, Grok Bot or Claude Cowork. For memory that grows with you, try Hermes Agent.

Meta Muse is one of the most talked about launches of the year. Within two weeks of its 8 September release, CNBC reported the Muse app ranking ahead of ChatGPT on Apple’s App Store.

Plenty of people still can’t use it, or don’t want to. There are three common reasons:

  • You live outside the US. Muse launched for US users aged 18 and over only.
  • You don’t want Meta holding your inbox, calendar and bank data. Muse trains on your chats by default unless you switch it off, according to Meta’s own help page. A WIRED reporter also found it kept asking for more personal data.
  • You want agents for your work, not your errands. Muse is built for bookings, forms and shopping, not for writing code or running projects.

Here are seven alternatives, and which reason each one solves.

Facts for the hosted products and the open source projects were checked on their official pages and GitHub repos in September 2026.

1. OpenClaw: the closest thing to Muse that you own #

OpenClaw is an open source personal assistant (MIT licence) that runs on your own computer. Like Muse inside WhatsApp, it replies where you already chat: WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, iMessage and more than 20 other channels. Your state, memory and logins stay on your hardware, and you can swap models freely, local ones included. There is no paid tier.

Solves: the US only limit and the data worry. The catch: by default tools run straight on your machine, so set up sandboxing before you hand it your accounts.

2. Munder Difflin: a team of work agents on your own computer #

Munder Difflin is what we make, and it answers the third reason. It will not book your travel. It gives you an office of AI agents for real work, mostly engineering: each agent is a terminal session running an engine you choose, such as Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor or Grok’s own CLI, or a local model through Ollama.

Each agent gets a desk, an inbox, memory in plain markdown files and a shared task board. You can start work from Slack or a webhook, schedule missions, and talk to one lead agent instead of twelve. A circuit breaker stops any agent that loops or overspends. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, in any country, and it is free and open source.

Solves: all three reasons, if your need is work rather than errands. The catch: agents run while your computer runs. For overnight work, keep a machine awake, like a Mac mini on your desk.

3. ChatGPT Work: the hosted pick for work #

ChatGPT Work is OpenAI’s agent inside ChatGPT. Plugins connect it to Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Drive, email and calendars. Scheduled Tasks run on a timer or when something happens, and you decide what needs your approval. In the Mac and Windows desktop app it can also work with your local files. It is available in the countries OpenAI supports.

Solves: the US only limit. The catch: your work runs through OpenAI’s cloud, and you need a ChatGPT plan that includes it.

4. Gemini Spark: for people who live in Google #

Gemini Spark suits you if your life already runs on Gmail, Drive and Calendar. Google says it keeps working with your phone and laptop off and checks with you before major actions. It comes with Google AI Pro and Ultra in select countries, plus select business users.

Solves: the US only limit, in the countries Google has opened. The catch: you are trading one big tech company for another.

5. Grok Bot: hosted teammates for your job #

Grok Bot from SpaceXAI is the most direct rival to Muse, aimed at work instead of errands. Bots sign into your apps through a normal browser, hand jobs to each other and keep going with your laptop shut. It comes with SuperGrok and paid Cursor plans, starting at $20 a month. All the Bots on your account share one cloud computer in the US. See our Meta Muse vs Grok Bot comparison for the full picture.

Solves: the work reason. The catch: there is no model picker, and your data lives on US cloud computers.

6. Claude Cowork: for work in your own folders #

Claude Cowork needs a paid Claude plan. In the macOS or Windows desktop app, Claude works only inside the folders you choose and asks before deleting anything. By default the work runs on Anthropic’s servers, which makes it closer to Muse than it looks, with a tighter fence around your files.

Solves: the work reason, and part of the data worry. The catch: it is still a hosted service.

7. Hermes Agent: for memory that grows #

Hermes Agent from Nous Research is open source (MIT). It curates its own memory, writes new skills after hard tasks and runs scheduled jobs. You talk to it over Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp or Signal. It can live on your laptop or a cheap server, and one command switches between model providers.

Solves: the US only limit and the data worry. The catch: you run it, so keeping it secure and updated is your job.

How to choose #

  • Want Muse, but you are outside the US? Start with OpenClaw.
  • Want errands handled but don’t trust Meta with your data? OpenClaw or Hermes Agent, with a local model if you want nothing to leave your machine.
  • Want agents that do your work, not your shopping? Download Munder Difflin. It is free and open source. Our install guide walks you through it.

FAQ

Why can't I use Meta Muse?

At launch on 8 September 2026 Muse was open only to people in the US aged 18 and over. Meta says more countries will be added over time but has not given dates.

Is there an open source alternative to Meta Muse?

Yes. OpenClaw is the closest: an open source personal assistant that runs on your own computer and replies in WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, iMessage and more. For work agents rather than errands, Munder Difflin is free and open source and runs a team of agents on your machine.

Does Meta Muse train on my data?

By default, yes. Meta's help page says Muse conversations can be used to train its models unless you turn that off in Settings, and that Meta removes details like names, emails and phone numbers first. Meta says Muse does not share your chats or VM data with its ad systems.

What is the best free alternative to Meta Muse?

For personal errands, OpenClaw and Hermes Agent are free and open source; you pay only for the AI model, or nothing with a local one. For work agents, Munder Difflin is free and uses the AI engines you already have.
